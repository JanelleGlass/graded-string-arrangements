# Graded String Arrangements (website)

Janelle's free public website: hymns and carols arranged for violin, viola, cello and bass in
levels from open strings to advanced (four levels and three voices; songs built before 2026-10-16
have six levels), each with a play-along reader. A few simple pieces have no levels: one line, the
same for each instrument it is for (Twinkle Harmony, 2026-10-21). Janelle is a Suzuki strings teacher, not a
programmer: tell her what changed on the page, not in the code.

- Live: https://graded-string-arrangements.vercel.app
- Repo: https://github.com/JanelleGlass/graded-string-arrangements (public: never commit anything
  that isn't meant for the website, such as PDFs for sale, scans, or her build tools)
- Hosting: Vercel, connected to this repo. **A push to `main` goes live by itself in about a
  minute.** So push only when Janelle has seen the change and said to.
- Vercel builds each commit once. A commit already pushed on another branch (a Claude working branch gets a
  Preview) is skipped when the same commit then reaches `main`, so the site doesn't change (2026-10-21, and
  probably the "Vercel missed the previous push" on 2026-10-17). To go live from a working branch, put a new
  commit on `main` (`git merge --no-ff`), not a fast-forward, and check Vercel's Deployments list shows it as
  Production.

This folder normally sits inside her `Music Arrangements` folder (as `website/`), where the
arrangements are built. Arrangement rules live in `../BUILD-SPEC.md`, not here.

## What's where

| Path | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `reader/reader.html` | The master reader: one file of HTML, CSS and JavaScript (alphaTab 1.8.4 from jsdelivr). Edit the reader here and only here. It is also the private Claude preview (see below), so it starts at `<style>` with no `<html>`/`<head>`. |
| `songs.json` | One entry per song. Its `section` puts it under a heading on the home page (Christmas, Hymns, Solos, Other: Janelle, 2026-11-02; the order is `SECTIONS` in `tools/build_site.py`); within a section, cards keep `songs.json` order. Other holds Twinkle Harmony and the sea shanties (Drunken Sailor, The Wellerman, each card labelled "Sea Shanty"). Fields are explained at the top of `tools/build_site.py`. |
| `songs/<slug>/<slug>.xml` | Each song's score: the finished MusicXML, copied from the Music Arrangements folder. 40 parts (levels and voices), or 42 or 44 when the song also has a violin and viola Countermelody and/or Concert parts (the Epic has both, 44; Joy to the World has Concert parts, 42), and any of those may end with a part named "Piano" (Silent Night, 45); songs built on 2026-10-16 have 34 (no cello or bass Inner) and songs built before have 24 (six tiers), and the reader handles all of them. A piece with no levels has one part per instrument it is for, all with the same line's name (Twinkle Harmony: "Violin — Harmony" to "Bass — Harmony"). |
| `songs/<slug>/index.html` | Generated by the build. Never edit by hand. |
| `index.html` | Home page, written by hand, except the tabs and song sections (each heading and its cards) between `<!-- songs:start -->` and `<!-- songs:end -->` and the app script between `<!-- app:start -->` and `<!-- app:end -->`, which the build writes. Tabs over the list (Janelle, 2026-11-02): "All songs", then one per section; a tab shows only its section, and the address remembers it (`/#hymns`), so a link can open on a tab and Back from a song returns to it. On phones the tabs sit in a grid, three to a row, so none is left alone on a line. The small script that switches them is at the end of the page. |
| `assets/` | Music font (Bravura) and string sounds (GeneralUser GS), with licences, and the site icons. Shared by every song. The piano sound (GeneralUser GS's Grand Piano, `assets/soundfont/piano-generaluser-sf2.b64.txt`, 2.9 MB) is loaded only by a song with a Piano part, added to the strings. |
| `tools/sf2_subset.py` | Cuts presets out of a SoundFont as base64 text: how the piano sound was made, from the full GeneralUser GS bank (https://github.com/mrbumpy409/GeneralUser-GS, 32 MB, not kept here). |
| `manifest.webmanifest` | Lets phones add the site to the home screen as an app that opens without the address bar (icons: `assets/icon-*.png`, `assets/apple-touch-icon.png`). | `app/app.js` | The app layer every page loads: starts the offline copy, lets iPhones play through the ringer switch, and shows the home page's "Install the app" button where the browser offers one. Kept out of the reader on purpose (see "The app version" below). |
| `app/together.js` | Play together on the website: the teacher's sign-in box, Follow on the home page, following the teacher from song to song, and the room through Supabase Realtime that the reader's conductor uses there (see "Play together on the website"). Song pages load it. |
| `sw.js` | The offline copy (service worker). Generated by the build from `tools/sw_template.js`. Never edit by hand. |
| `tools/build_site.py` | Builds the song pages, the home-page cards and `sw.js`. |
| `tests/smoke.py`, `tests/mockroom.js` | Browser test of the site, the conductor and the app version (install, offline, updates). |
| `.vercelignore` | Keeps the working files above off the live site. |

Commands (on Janelle's Windows PC use `python`; elsewhere `python3`):

    python tools/build_site.py
    python tests/smoke.py

The test checks the Parts panel on the first 24-part and the first 40-part song in `songs.json`,
on the first 34-part song (or, with none, the 40-part song's score cut to 34 parts), on the
first piece with no levels (one line), and on the first song with a Piano part.

The whole test takes about twelve minutes (eight songs, 2026-10-23), a little more with each song, and says how
long it took. Run it with no time limit (a Claude session: in the background). A limit that stops it part-way (a
`timeout`, or the 10-minute limit on one command) closes its browser, and Playwright then reports "Page crashed" or
"Target crashed" in whatever step was running. That looks like the reader crashing, but it isn't: on 2026-10-23 three
runs stopped that way at 9 min 50 s, in Joy to the World's Concert checks, and passed with no limit.

In a Claude cloud session the network may block cdn.jsdelivr.net (2026-11-02): then every song page fails "page
loads (TimeoutError)", the reader never having got alphaTab. Fetch the same file from npm and point the test at it:
`npm pack @coderline/alphatab@1.8.4` in the scratchpad, untar `package/dist/alphaTab.min.js`, and run the test with
`ALPHATAB_JS=<that path>`. Python's Playwright there must match the browser already installed (`pip install
playwright==` the version `node -e "console.log(require('playwright/package.json').version)"` prints).

The test needs Playwright once per computer: `python -m pip install playwright` then
`python -m playwright install chromium`. To look at a page yourself: `python -m http.server 8000`
in this folder, then http://localhost:8000/ .

## Adding a song

Only after Janelle has checked the finished arrangement in the Music Arrangements folder.

1. Copy the finished file to `songs/<slug>/<slug>.xml` (slug: lowercase-with-dashes, e.g.
   `holy-holy-holy`). Copy only the finished arrangement (the 40-part file, with its Countermelody, Concert and
   Piano parts when it has them), nothing else from her folder. Convert it to LF line endings first: the files in her
   folder are often CRLF, and the build fingerprints raw bytes. A solo piece goes on only when Janelle asks for that
   piece (so far Morning Has Broken's two solo viola Variations, 2026-10-23): its XML only, as one line, with its part
   renamed from `Viola` to `Viola — Solo` in the site copy so the build reads it, then colored and fingered with her
   `add-colors-fingerings.py` (her solo files are plain, and the reader's finger looks need fingerings; that tool
   writes CRLF, so convert to LF again). Its PDF stays private.
2. Add the song to `songs.json` (title, section on the home page: Christmas, Hymns, Solos (a piece for one player)
   or Other; category, a small label on its card, or "" where it would only repeat the section; collection, source
   line, `pdf_url` empty until there is a Payhip link). A new section goes in `SECTIONS` in `tools/build_site.py`.
3. Run the build. It reads key, time, bars and the written tempo from the score, and stops unless the
   parts are one of the layouts the reader knows, named and ordered as in BUILD-SPEC section 3:
   40 parts in levels and voices (level by level; Melody, Inner, Bass Line; violin, viola, cello,
   bass), the same 40 then a Countermelody on violin and viola and/or a Concert part on each (42 or 44),
   34 in the same with no cello or bass Inner (built 2026-10-16), or 24 in the old six tiers
   (level by level; violin, viola, cello, bass), or one line (a piece with no levels: one to four
   parts, violin to bass, each named "<instrument> — <the line's name>"). It says which one it read. Tell Janelle what it read so she can catch a mismatch. It also
   adds the song to the offline copy (`sw.js`), so installed copies get it on their next open.
4. Run the test. Show her the page (local server, laptop and phone sizes) before pushing.
5. Commit and push when she says go. Then check the live page loads.

## Changing the reader

1. Edit `reader/reader.html`.
2. Build, then test. For anything visual, also take screenshots at laptop (1366x860), phone
   landscape (844x390, 667x375 for iPhone SE) and phone portrait sizes, and look at them.
3. Update the Claude preview: publish `reader/reader.html` as an artifact to
   https://claude.ai/artifact/WqVvah4mND1xSPHvPpybTv (pass that URL so it updates in place).
   Don't change its capabilities: it uses the `room` capability for the conductor. Its score,
   font and sound files are already published there and are kept. The preview plays the
   four-level First Noel, so publish a copy of the reader with its two "six levels" lines (the
   meta description and the About box) worded as the build words them for a song in levels and voices; the
   master reader keeps "six levels" because the build swaps those lines song by song. In that copy the Print
   button is `hidden` too: Claude's preview window can't open a print dialog.
4. Push when Janelle says go.

If a reader change touches one of the song-specific strings the build swaps out (title, About box
facts, `SCORE_URL`, `BASE_TEMPO`, `TEMPO_PERCENT`, the storage key, the Buy-note line, the title wrapper), update
`TEMPLATE` and `song_page()` in `tools/build_site.py` too. The build stops with a clear message if
one goes missing.

## Play together on the website (2026-11-10)

- No class code (Janelle: "is it necessary to have to type in a student code?"; she's fine with anyone on the same
  piece seeing it). Every song page is one room and joins it by itself 1.5 s after loading. While a teacher conducts
  that piece, a small "Follow" appears at the top of the music, left of Print ("Following", teal, once on; tap to
  stop). Nothing follows unless it is tapped. The teacher signs in from the About box ("Teacher? Sign in to
  conduct", with her violin-studio email and password, her studio app's account); signed in, "Conduct" is in the
  strip on her device only, and the box has "Start conducting" and "Sign out". The first version (same day) had a
  class code typed into a "Play together" box; its saved code is cleared.
- Backend: her Supabase project `violin-studio` (wjzzdiuenlisxueaxqjq), Realtime only. `public.conduct_classes`
  lists who may conduct (its `code` column is no longer used). Each song is two private channels: `conduct:<slug>`,
  where only a listed teacher may send (policies on `realtime.messages` check it), and `conduct-in:<slug>`, where
  anyone may send presence and clock pings. So a student can't move anyone's music. The teacher says she's
  conducting every 2 s on her channel; a follower drops her after 7 s of silence.
- The publishable key is in `app/together.js` on purpose (it is public; the database rules guard everything).
  supabase-js is 2.118.0 from jsdelivr. Offline, a page has no room and tries again when the internet comes back.
- Free plan limits: about 200 devices at once (every open song page is one) and 100 messages a second for the
  project; a class of 20 uses about 40 a second while playing. `eventsPerSecond: 60` lifts the script's own limit
  of 10 a device (with 10, a second student starting to follow at once missed the start).
- Changing songs (Janelle, 2026-11-10: "when the conducting device switches songs, it will switch for everyone else if
  they press follow ... even if they are on the home page"). The teacher's conducting carries on from page to page in
  her tab (sessionStorage `gsa-conduct`) until she taps "Conducting" (or "Stop" on the home page); every page, the home
  page too, listens on `conduct:__site`, where her device says every 2 s which song it has open (none on the home page).
  Tapping Follow keeps a tab following (`gsa-follow`) until "Following" is tapped, the teacher stops, or she isn't heard
  for 10 s: when she opens another song the tab goes there and follows by itself once its player is ready. The home
  page shows a small Follow at the top right while she conducts (which takes the tab to her song), "Following, waiting
  for the teacher to choose a song" while she is on the home page, and on her device "Conducting: open a song · Stop".
  A phone or iPad keeps a new page's sound asleep until it is touched, and the music can't move until then, so the
  page shows a big "Tap to follow your teacher" card; any tap wakes it and catches up. (Headless Chromium never
  sleeps; the test fakes it with a wrapped AudioContext.) A follower that arrives and asks the time before the teacher's
  device has seen it arrive is answered anyway, and asks again every 3 s until answered (it once never lined up).
- Tests use their own channels (`conduct:test-...`) when localStorage has `gsa-together-test` (tests/smoke.py sets it):
  on 2026-11-10 a local test on Silent Night met Janelle conducting it on the live site, and its students followed her.
- Testing needs a teacher account: make a throwaway one in `auth.users` with a row in `conduct_classes`, and delete it
  afterwards (done that way on 2026-11-10).

## The app version (home screen, offline)

Added to a home screen ("Install the app" in Chrome, Edge and Android; Share, then Add to Home Screen
on iPhone and iPad), the site opens like an app. After one visit every song works with no internet.

- The offline part lives in `app/app.js` and `sw.js`, wired in by the build. The reader itself has no
  app code, so `reader/reader.html` and the Claude preview are untouched. Keep it that way: the same
  layer will be reused for the app-store versions (Google Play, then the App Store through
  Capacitor), where `app/app.js` skips the offline copy because the app carries its own files.
- What is kept offline: the home page, every song page and score, the Bravura font, the string
  sounds, the icons, alphaTab from jsdelivr, and the Google Fonts lettering (Latin letters). About 8 MB
  with two songs. Each new song adds its score (1 to 3 MB). Once that passes about 50 MB, switch to
  keeping only the songs a student has opened.
- Updates: the build writes a fingerprint of every file into `sw.js`. After a push, an installed copy
  picks up the change the next time it is opened online, and downloads only the files that changed
  (a new song doesn't re-download the 4 MB of sounds). Pages come fresh from the network when it
  answers within 3.5 seconds, so a new song shows up on the home page straight away.
- Score, font and sound addresses carry their fingerprint (`?v=...`), so a page never mixes an old
  score with a new page.
- iPhone: `navigator.audioSession.type = 'playback'` lets the music play with the ringer switch off
  (iOS 17 and later). Older iPhones stay silent on mute; there's nothing more to do there without a
  native app.
- App icon: the white note on teal (`assets/icon-*.png`, `assets/apple-touch-icon.png`), the same as
  the browser-tab icon. Janelle chose it over a design with the open strings in their colors (2026-10-16).
- `sw.js` lists every song page's fingerprint. When sessions commit separately, whoever commits second
  rebuilds and commits `sw.js` with the song pages.
- The Claude desktop browser pane supports offline copies. Use the `website-app-8030` entry in
  `../.claude/launch.json`: the other entries hard-code ports that other sessions are often using.

## Decisions Janelle has made about the reader (keep them)

- Line view: the music starts at the left edge with no lead-in space and the clef is never cut off;
  the playing note sits in the middle of the screen (Janelle, 2026-10-21; it sat a third of the way
  from the left before). Music is
  top-aligned under the header. The start of the line (each part's name, its clef and the key
  signature) stays pinned at the left edge as the music slides under it, so students can look back at
  the key signature; when the key changes, the pinned key changes as the new key reaches the edge.
  It is a copy of the start as drawn, so at the start it sits exactly on it. When it is wider than
  half the screen (big music), the playing note sits just past it instead (Janelle, 2026-10-17).
- Pages: every line of music names its parts beside the staves, not just the first line (2026-10-17).
- Every setting is always visible in a strip at the bottom, on phones too, never covering notes.
  Phone-landscape buttons are compact so the strip fits one row on regular iPhones.
- Everything stays in the same place so people can orient themselves: a choice that doesn't apply
  right now is greyed out in its place (a tap says why), never hidden, and a tap-to-change button
  keeps one width (its longest label) so nothing beside it shifts (Janelle, 2026-10-16).
- The size of the music (− / +, 0.15x to 1.75x; below 0.3x since 2026-10-23, so a whole piece fits on a laptop or
  iPad screen, with the counts shrinking in proportion there) and, on bigger screens, Line or Pages sit at the top
  right of the music, in the band with the Strings key, so they never cover notes (moved there from
  the strip on 2026-10-16). The band always keeps its room, even with string colors off. Scrolling down
  the page, they stay at the top of the window, over the music (Janelle wanted them always in reach,
  2026-10-17); the Strings key scrolls away with the music. While Parts
  is open they slide over with the panel's edge and sit just beside it, never under it (Janelle found
  them hidden by the panel, 2026-10-17). Where that leaves too little room beside the Strings key (a
  tablet held upright), the key and the buttons take their phone size; then the key drops the word
  "Strings" and keeps its coloured letters; and with no room at all (a small phone with the Hear
  speakers out) the key steps aside until the panel closes. The music doesn't move for any of this.
- What's drawn on the page has no panel: each setting is its own tap-to-change button in the strip,
  stepping through its choices and showing a toast, the same buttons on every screen: string colors
  (All, 1st, Off), On the note, and Counting (on, off). On the note steps through five looks, each
  label saying what's on the notes: "Fingers inside" → "Fingers to the left" →
  "Note names and fingers" (names inside, numbers to the left, since only one thing fits inside a note head) →
  "Note names" → "Plain notes". Numbers beside a note go on its left (Janelle chose it on 2026-11-02 after comparing
  "Fingers to the right" and "Fingers to the left" side by side: on the right a dotted note's dot could read as a 0
  beside the number, or look busy; a choice saved as the right becomes the left). A number goes before the note's
  sharp or flat. A bar's first
  note has only the fixed room after the bar line, so a high note's number ran into the bar number ("13" and a 1 read
  as 131) and an arrow onto the bar line: after drawing, the reader finds those notes and asks alphaTab for two
  fingerings of its own there ("4" and "i", or the wider "m" with an arrow), which keeps room before the note; they
  are never shown (placeFingers hides them) and the number goes in their place. Paper has less of that room than the
  screen, so it is checked again for paper and undone after printing. Not on a phone's screen: the fingerings make
  the line a little taller and a phone fits its line to the screen's height, so the music shrank a step for one look. Finger numbers and note names show on every note (no "first time
  only"). The music stays the same size for all six looks, Plain notes too. Phones start on
  "Note names and fingers", laptops on "Fingers inside" (note names kept separately for each).
  Each look's little note head and words sit together in the middle of the button (black head for
  Plain notes, blue otherwise).
  History (2026-10-16): the "On the page" panel became separate buttons, then Note Names, Fingers
  and placement buttons on every screen, which Janelle folded into this one sequence.
- Parts: a quiet bump-out tab on the right edge of the screen, halfway down the music (a chevron
  and "Parts", read sideways, in soft grey; 34px wide, 136px tall), with swoopy sides: its top and
  bottom curve smoothly into the screen edge (and into the panel's edge when open). It slides a
  panel in from the right, from the top of the screen down to the strip. There is no Parts button in the strip. The tab rides along the panel's edge while it's
  open and closes it again (so does ×). Closed, the music runs a little under the tab (it
  overlaps the music by about half its width), on a wide screen too, past the centred page. Open, the panel slides over the music, which stays where it
  is and runs on behind it (Janelle's choice: an earlier version moved the music over beside the
  panel). Pointing at the tab names the part on the page, or "3 parts". Phones show short
  level names ("Adv. melody") to keep the panel narrow. The panel holds one table: a
  level on each row and an eye for each instrument (See), headed Vln, Vla, C, B (Janelle's
  choice: C and B, not Vc and Cb; the staff labels use the same: "Vln", "Vla", "C", "B"). Every part is on the page or not on its
  own, so a violin at Open Strings can read with a cello at Advanced Melody. Parts show in score
  order; one part always stays on the page. The table scrolls, fading at the bottom, when the
  screen is short.
- Same notes: under each level's name, a quiet grey line says which of its parts play the same notes
  (same rhythm and note names, so the tune an octave lower counts), in plain words, one line per
  group, in small type: "all parts have the same notes" (Janelle's wording), or "violin & bass have
  the same notes" over "viola & cello have the same notes"; a level where every
  part has its own line shows nothing. Not "Vln = B": Janelle found that it reads like a note name. Worked out
  from each score as it loads, so a new song needs nothing listed; only exact matches count.
  Pointing at a part names its match. (Corner dots with a key came first; Janelle found them messy
  and distracting, 2026-10-16.) Open Strings never shows the line or the hint: every part there is
  open strings, so Janelle found it unnecessary (2026-10-17).
- Parts for a song in levels and voices (Janelle approved it 2026-10-16): a
  row for each level and voice, headed "Level & voice": Open Strings, Easy Melody, Easy Inner, Easy
  Bass Line, then Melody, Inner, Bass Line (the middle level has no word, as in the part names), then
  the Advanced ones, with a hairline between levels. Phones say "Easy melody", "Adv. bass line".
  Since 2026-10-17 every instrument has every voice (40 parts), so a cello or bass choir can play:
  every cell has an eye. A song built on 2026-10-16 (34 parts) has no cello or bass Inner part, so
  those cells are greyed out in their places (faint, dashed), and a tap says "No Inner part for cello
  in this piece: cellos and basses play a Bass Line or a Melody". One quiet
  line at the top of the table gives the group rule: "One player on Melody, the lowest player on Bass
  Line, everyone else on Inner"; it scrolls away with the rows on a short screen. The reader reads the
  layout from the score's part names, and saved choices move to their match: from the six levels by
  the table in BUILD-SPEC section 3 (and back), from 34 to 40 parts by name. The About box says "Four
  levels for violin, viola, cello and bass, each with a melody, an inner part and a bass line." 24-part
  songs look as they always did.
- Countermelody and Concert parts (the Epic, 2026-10-18, and Joy to the World's Concert parts the same day; Janelle asked for them on the site): a row for each at
  the bottom of the table, below a hairline, named "Countermelody" and "Concert" on phones too. Eyes for violin and
  viola; the cello and bass cells are greyed out in place, and a tap says "No Countermelody part for cello: it is
  written for violin and viola". Each row's description says what it is (the Concert part: played with the group by
  a teacher or an advanced player). The Concert parts have no string colours or finger numbers, as in her files:
  with only Concert parts on the page the Strings key steps aside, and tapping String colors or a finger look says
  "(Concert parts have no string colors)" / "(Concert parts have no finger numbers)", as Open Strings already did
  for fingers. Their double stops get a note name on every note, and their second voice (the Epic's Violin Concert tune
  under its drone in the Celtic section; Joy's held drone under the tune) gets names, counts and the start ring
  too: a part's counts follow the voice with the most notes in each bar, and where both voices start a note
  together, tapping one rings the note tapped. A song may have the Countermelody, the Concert parts, or both (42 or 44
  parts). The About box adds ", plus a countermelody and a concert part for violin and viola" (or just the one it
  has). The group rule line is unchanged: these parts are extras outside it. Saved choices on such a song store the
  layout as `voices40` with the full id in `partsFull` (the graded 40 keep their numbers), so an offline copy from
  before the extra rows keeps a student's graded parts instead of reading an unknown layout as the six levels and
  swapping them (found 2026-10-18; keep it).
- Piano part (Silent Night, 2026-11-02: Janelle asked for a piano part, then "Add it to the site"): the hymn as the
  hymnal prints it, on two staves, as the last part of the score, named "Piano". One row at the bottom of the Parts
  table, named "Piano", below a hairline, with a single eye (and in the Hear mix a single speaker) across the four
  instrument columns, since it is one part, not one per instrument. Its staff is labelled "Pno"; no string colours or
  finger numbers, like the Concert parts, and String colors says "(Piano parts have no string colors)". The About box
  adds ", and a piano part" (", plus a piano part" with no other extras); the footnote says "The string and piano
  sounds". Its page loads the piano sound after the strings (a page without a piano never downloads it). Its name sits
  left of the brace, where alphaTab lets it run past the music's left edge, so with the Piano on the page the reader
  widens the left margin by just that much (`fitNames`, `nameRoom.left`); every other song keeps the music flush left.
  The score numbers each staff's voices 1 and 2: alphaTab draws a staff's voice 5 as a second voice, in grey.
- Pieces with no levels (Twinkle Harmony, 2026-10-21: Janelle asked for "no extra parts, just viola and cello
  playing these same notes", then the same part for violin and bass): one row in the Parts table, named for the
  line ("Harmony"), headed "Part"; eyes for the instruments it is for, any others greyed out in place (a tap says
  "No Harmony part for violin: this piece is for viola and cello"); no group rule. A first visit shows and plays

- The music on the screen keeps time with the speaker (Janelle asked, 2026-10-22: over Bluetooth, and with her iPad
  mirrored to an LG TV, the sound came after the notes lit; Soundslice does this). The player reports a moment as
  soon as it hands the sound to the browser, about a fifth of a second before even the device's own speaker plays
  it (measured in Chromium: 0.22 s early before this), and Bluetooth or a TV adds more. So everything that moves with
  the music (lit notes, cursor, sliding line, counts, count-in numbers) waits by what the browser reports: its own
  buffering plus the speaker's delay (Safari from iOS 18.4, Chrome, Edge, Firefox; Safari's Private Browsing reports
  no speaker delay). Only the picture waits: the sound, loops and count-ins are timed as before. A pause keeps the
  beat last heard, and Play starts again there. In Play together a student's screen keeps time with the teacher's
  speaker. No manual setting yet: if a speaker or TV reports its delay wrongly, a manual one like Soundslice's
  "Audio latency" is the next step. alphaTab's .min.js shortens its own internal names, so the reader measures
  through the browser's audio node and alphaTab's public events, never alphaTab's underscore names.
- Metronome is one button that cycles Off → On → On with count-in → Count-in only.
  A count-in before a pickup counts the missing beats of its bar ("1 2 3 1 2" before a one-beat pickup in 3/4). The counts and the start ring skip the gap. A count-in started anywhere in the first bar counts from the top of it, like any other bar.
- Bouncing ball (Janelle, 2026-11-05/06: for young students who can't read rhythms yet and don't know when the next
  note comes): a small teal ball above each part's staff (one per part on the page), clear of the stems, beams and bow
  marks, and never up into the counts of the part above (its bounces stay below them too). It bounces once on every
  count, landing as the count is heard (a ball reads as the beat, so landing early would teach clapping off the beat;
  the eye sees where it's going while it flies), and on every note as it starts: on a held note it travels forward a bounce at
  a time (a half note two bounces, a whole note four), each landing a share of the way to the next note in proportion
  to the time (Janelle, 2026-11-06: it bounced in place before), then lands on the next note, higher for a longer flight. Tied notes are one
  note. Over a rest it is a hollow ring; rests in a row are one. Stopped, it sits on the note Play starts from; a loop's
  last flight goes back to its first note; to the next line on pages, it flies off the end of the line and drops onto
  the next. It keeps time with the speaker. On by default, saved. Its switch is a round button in the strip, after
