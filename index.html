How it works

  1. Choose your partYour instrument and your level. Notes are colored by string, with finger numbers.
  2. Press PlayThe music scrolls and the notes pulse with the beat. Choose who plays along with you and set the tempo.
  3. Practise a tricky spotDrag across bars (on a phone, hold and drag) to loop them, with a count-in each time if you like.

Full screen on a phone or tablet: add this site to your home screen (in your browser’s Share or ⋮ menu, choose Add to Home Screen). It then opens like an app, without the address bar, and every piece plays without the internet.

Levels and voices

Pieces are arranged in four levels. Above Open Strings, each level has three voices, a Melody, an Inner part and a Bass Line, so any duet or trio has a real bass, even without a cello.

1
Open Strings
Plucked open strings, one on each new chord. No fingers needed. It plays as a bass line, so it fits any group.
2
Easy
First position, no low or high fingers, and longer notes: the tune’s quick notes are folded in.
3
Intermediate
The tune as written, with chord tones under it. On the song page these parts are simply Melody, Inner and Bass Line.
4
Advanced
Into third position: the tune as a variation in eighth notes, a descant or countermelody, and a moving bass.
  • MelodyViolin, viola, cello, bassThe tune. Cello and bass play it lower.
  • InnerViolin, violaFills in the chord between the tune and the bass.
  • Bass LineViolin, viola, cello, bassThe bass notes from the hymnal, so a violin or viola can be the bass in a duet.

Playing together? One player on Melody, the lowest player on Bass Line, everyone else on Inner. A second cello or bass doubles the Bass Line. Levels mix freely: a violin on Easy Melody can play with a viola on Advanced Bass Line.

Older pieces still have six levels for now, each for all four instruments: Open Strings, Easy, Melody, Harmony, Advanced Melody and Obbligato. A piece’s About box says which kind it has.

Free to read and play along. Playback is synthesized, so treat it as a guide for notes and timing rather than a model for tone.
