# Graded String Arrangements

A free, public website: hymns and carols arranged in six levels for violin, viola, cello and bass,
with a play-along reader for each piece. Live at https://graded-string-arrangements.vercel.app

## What's here

- `index.html` – the home page, with the list of pieces.
- `songs/<piece>/index.html` – the reader for one piece (generated); `songs/<piece>/<piece>.xml` is its score.
- `assets/` – shared by every piece: the music font (Bravura) and the string sounds (GeneralUser GS),
  each with its licence file, and the site icons.
- `manifest.webmanifest` – lets a phone add the site to its home screen, where it opens like an app.
- `app/app.js`, `sw.js` – the offline copy: after one visit every piece works without the internet.
  `sw.js` is generated by the build.
- `reader/reader.html` – the reader every song page is built from.
- `songs.json`, `tools/build_site.py` – the song list and the script that builds the pages from it.
- `tests/smoke.py` – a browser test of the home page, every song, the conductor, and the app version
  (install, offline, updates).

The site itself is plain static files; Vercel serves the folder as it is (the working files above are
left out by `.vercelignore`). `CLAUDE.md` has the full how-to.

## Sight-reading (unlisted for now)

`sight-reading/` is a page of endless reading exercises for violin and viola, page by page with a sight-reading book in
the works: `generator.js` makes each exercise from a book page's rules (`pages.json`, four placeholder pages for now),
and the reader plays it. Look ahead (a button in its strip) makes each note vanish a little before it sounds, so the
eyes stay ahead of the hands. `sight-reading/flash/` is Flash a bar: a short pattern shows, hides, and the student plays
it from that glance, with a streak and levels; with Listen on, the microphone marks each note right or wrong, early or
late (worked out in the browser; nothing is recorded or saved). Duet (a button in its row) adds a second part on the
same chords, for violin and viola in any pairing, at the same page, on open strings, at an earlier page or harder for a
teacher; one link gives the same duet on every device, each showing the part it plays. `tests/listening.py` checks the
listening. `tests/sight_reading.py` checks 1,000 exercises per page and instrument against the rules, and the duets.

## Duo practice (two tablets at home)

Two people practising together at home, each on their own tablet: one leads (picks the piece, presses Play, sets the
tempo, the bars and the count-in), the other follows, and each tablet shows its own part.

How to set one up (the teacher, no code):

1. On any song page, tap the title, then "Teacher? Sign in to conduct", and sign in with the studio account.
2. Tap "Duo logins". Type a username (letters, numbers, dots or dashes, for example `practice-duo`) and a password of at
   least 6 characters, and tap "Make duo login".
3. Send the two people the username, the password and this link: https://graded-string-arrangements.vercel.app/?duo
4. On each tablet they open the link once and sign in, ticking "This tablet leads" on one of them. From then on the
   site opens connected: a bar at the top says "Duo · Leading" or "Duo · Following", and whether the other tablet is
   there. The follower's tablet goes to whatever piece the leader opens; after it opens a piece, one tap on the screen
   lets it play.
5. To swap who leads, tap the bar and choose "I lead" or "I follow": the other tablet swaps too.

"New password" (on the same Duo logins screen) gives a duo a new password and signs both tablets out; "Remove" ends it.

A duo login is not a studio account and can't see anything in the studio app. It is a row in the Supabase table
`gsa_duos` (a scrambled password and a secret room name), reached only through the `gsa-duo` function
(`tools/supabase/gsa-duo/index.ts`). The tablets need the internet; the screens stay on while a duo is signed in.

## Adding a piece

1. Put its 24-part score at `songs/<new-piece>/<new-piece>.xml`.
2. Add it to `songs.json`.
3. `python tools/build_site.py`, then `python tests/smoke.py`.
